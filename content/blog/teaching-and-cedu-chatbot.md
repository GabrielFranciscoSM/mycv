# Teaching and Building a Cedu Chatbot

Teaching for a year changed how I think about learning and product design. I worked one-on-one with a student for twelve months — planning sessions, breaking down concepts, and adapting explanations to fit their pace. That experience was humbling and clarifying: learning isn't a single path, it's a conversation.

During that time I began developing a small educational chatbot (I call it the "Cedu Chatbot") to help the student practice problems and review concepts between sessions. The chatbot is intentionally simple: short prompts, guided hints, and quick checks to keep confidence high and frustration low.

## What tutoring taught me

- Explain the goal first. Students ask "why" more than you expect; naming the objective keeps them engaged.
- Small wins matter. Short, solvable exercises build momentum.
- Feedback beats correctness. Showing why an answer is wrong — kindly — creates learning, not shame.

## Why a chatbot?

A chatbot extends the learning loop. It provides practice on demand, offers hints when someone is stuck, and keeps explanations consistent between sessions. It's not a replacement for human tutoring — it's a complement that scales attention when the tutor isn't available.

From a technical perspective, designing the Cedu Chatbot forced me to think about micro-interactions, prompt design, and how to surface uncertainty (when the bot should ask for clarification or suggest a human check). These are the same problems that matter in product design: trust, clarity, and graceful failure.

## Next steps

I plan to keep iterating on the bot: better hint strategies, clearer error messages, and small analytics to track progress (not to judge, but to inform the next lesson). Teaching for a year and building the bot made me appreciate the blend of empathy and engineering — both are necessary to make tools that actually help people learn.

Let's keep building tools that teach, not just test.
