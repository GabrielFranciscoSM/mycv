# Soft Skills That Ship Features

Talking about soft skills feels vague in a technical portfolio. But over time I noticed a pattern: projects with better conversations finish faster. Meeting notes, quick demos, and small decisions saved days of rework.

## Concrete habits that help teams

- Short demos every few days. They expose assumptions early and reduce scope creep.
- Commit messages that explain "why". Future you (and your teammates) will thank you.
- Ask clarifying questions instead of assuming requirements are fixed.

These aren't glamorous, but they remove friction. In one project, a ten-minute sync avoided a week-long integration headache because we agreed on one data format early.

## Communication as engineering

Good communication is a form of engineering: it reduces uncertainty, aligns expectations, and makes trade-offs explicit. It's also low-cost to practice — add a line to a PR explaining the intent, or record a 60-second walkthrough of a tricky bug.

Soft skills don't replace hard skills. They multiply them. If you can write a clean component and explain its limits clearly, that component will actually get used.

If you're building your portfolio or collaborating on a small team, invest a little time in the conversations. The code will follow.
